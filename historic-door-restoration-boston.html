Historic Door Restoration in Boston

Boston's historic neighborhoods — Back Bay, Beacon Hill, Jamaica Plain, South End, Cambridge — are full of original entry doors that are worth restoring rather than replacing. These doors were built from old-growth hardwood that no longer exists commercially: denser, more stable, and more durable than anything you can buy today. The goal of historic restoration is to preserve that material, repair what's damaged, and bring the finish and hardware back to original character.

Our landmark projects include a full restoration at 990 Centre Street in Jamaica Plain — a Victorian estate with an ornately carved entry door, original bronze hardware, and a threshold that had seen a century of New England winters. We stripped it to bare wood, hand-cleaned and re-lacquered every piece of the original hardware, patched and faired the carved panel areas, and applied a museum-quality Epifanes oil varnish finish that replicates the depth and warmth of the original.

We also completed the entry restoration at 71 Marlborough Street in Back Bay and an institutional entry at MIT in Cambridge — each project adapted to the building's period, the owner's requirements, and the finish standard the door demands.

Full Restoration Scope
  • Full chemical and mechanical strip to bare wood
  • Panel repair and wood patching
  • Carved detail cleaning and preservation
  • Original mortise hardware restoration
  • Hinge re-mortising and re-hanging
  • Penetrating oil finish + Epifanes varnish topcoat
  • Rotted sill and jamb repair
  • Full weatherstrip replacement
  • Frame alignment and door re-hanging
  • Threshold repair and finishing
Why Restore — Not Replace
  • Original hardwood is denser and more stable than modern replacements — a restored door outperforms a new one
  • Replacement destroys the original hardware mortises, frame, and profile — restoring preserves the whole assembly
  • Many Boston neighborhoods and condo associations require repair over replacement for historic properties
  • A properly restored and maintained door holds a museum-quality finish for 10+ years
